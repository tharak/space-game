# Successor Stars — fleet battle (Rust · Bevy · ECS)

A real-time fleet battle with hundreds of ships a side, in Rust with [Bevy 0.19](https://bevy.org) and its ECS. You command divisions, and formation decides the fight: a deep arrow can tear a hole in a thin line, a crescent can close on an arrow's flanks, and shots into a ship's flank or rear land far more often than shots into its front.

**Play:** <https://tharak.github.io/space-game/> (needs a browser with WebGL2)

## prototype01 — Fleet dominion

The start page's prototype, in real time (Pause, 1×, 2×, 4×). Code: `src/prototypes/dominion.rs`.

- **Planets:** some of the Voronoi regions hold a planet (Sites and Planets sliders). Each builds ships into its own garrison fleet: one every 15 s ÷ the planet's size (1–3), set by the Production slider.
- **Map:** planets and fleets are the sites of one multiplicatively weighted Voronoi diagram: a planet weighs size × Planet weight (0.5), a fleet ships × Weight per ship (0.1), so fleet cells move and a big fleet claims more space. Planet cells take the owner's colour (Planet cells toggle).
- **Highways:** the Delaunay triangulation of the planets (code: `src/prototypes/highways.rs`). Fleets only travel along its edges. A right-click goes to the nearest point on a highway, by the shortest route along the highways, so a fleet can stop halfway along a road to block it. The selected fleet's route shows as gold dashes.
- **Supply:** three resources, ammo, food and parts, with no global purse. Each point of a planet's size produces one kind, shown by the icons above the planet (ammo a red triangle, food a green disc, parts a grey square), into the planet's own stock at Income (4) × tax (20 %) a second. Home planets are size 3 and make all three. Every combat ship carries its own stores (up to 10 ammo, 3 food, 2 parts), split evenly within its fleet at each 5 s maintenance phase.
  - No ammo: the ship can't fire (each shot uses 0.1).
  - No food: each ship eats Food use (0.25) per phase, and a hungry ship deserts with a 25 % chance per phase.
  - No parts: out of combat, a part repairs Part repair (2.5) hull points; a damaged ship with no parts breaks up with a chance of 30 % × its damage.
  - Every shortage shakes the fleet's morale.
  - A fleet at one of its owner's planets fills up from the planet's stock. Elsewhere, supply ships bring it. Each carries one kind, with its icon drawn on the ship. For each kind, a planet holding half a Supply load (20) sends one to the nearest fleet (by highway) that is short of that kind.
  - The supply ship travels the highways and joins the fleet. It keeps as close behind it as it can, two ship spacings behind the rear rank, unloads as the fleet needs, and is used up when empty.
  - Supply ships never enter the other side's cells: a convoy waits at the edge until the way is clear, and a fleet's supply ships stop short. Own and independent cells are open to them.
  - They never fire and can be shot; the player can box-select them and send them into any fleet.
  - Planets build ships out of parts: Ship cost (5) per ship.
- **Rebellion:** the people of every Blue or Red planet run a port of NetLogo's *Rebellion* model (Epstein 2002, civil violence): 1120 agents and some cops on a 40×40 torus, two steps a second. An agent rebels when its grievance, hardship × (1 − legitimacy), minus its risk aversion × estimated arrest chance is above 0.1. Each cop jails one rebel in vision. The game sets three things. The cops are the owner's ships within twice the capture radius × Cops per ship (40) ÷ planet size. Legitimacy is 1 − tax − occupation: a conquest costs 0.3, fading over 60 s. War hardship (+0.3) applies while fighting rages nearby. Rebels strike, so income and building drop by the smoothed rebel share (10 s time constant, red arc). When that share passes Revolt (25 %), the planet goes independent with a rebel garrison of rebels × size × 10 ships. Each owned planet's people are drawn inside its circle as a pie (rebels red, prisoners dark, the quiet rest). The number under a planet is how many ships it needs nearby to stay calm: green when they are there, red when not, grey for an independent (what a conqueror would need), "!" when no garrison is enough. It comes from `src/prototypes/calm.rs`, a table of the fewest cops that keep the model calm by legitimacy and hardship, generated by `cargo run --release --example rebellion_sweep -- table`. Home planets are loyal to their own side and never rebel (a ring in the side's colour), unless the enemy takes them. Click a planet for its numbers; `[` and `]` change its tax by 5 %. At 10 % tax or less nobody rebels without occupation or war; at 20 % an abandoned planet revolts in about 10 s. `cargo run --release --example rebellion_sweep` prints the model's response to legitimacy, cops and war.
- **Captains:** code in `src/prototypes/captains.rs`. Only fleets led by a captain's flagship move: the flagship is the big white ship with a gold dot, and the captain's initial is drawn above the fleet. Ships without a captain stay as garrisons. Select them together with a captain's fleet nearby and right-click, and the captain takes them along. Captains never merge.
  - Each side starts with 3 captains (Fleets slider), each with a different skill:

    | Skill | Effect |
    |---|---|
    | Commander | +50% ships commanded |
    | Engineer | repairs with half the parts, half the wrecks |
    | Quartermaster | 30% less food, half the desertion |
    | Gunner | +25% rate of fire |
    | Sharpshooter | shots use half the ammo |
    | Navigator | +25% speed |
    | Inspiring | half the shock from shortages, crowding and losing the flagship |
    | Governor | ships count double as cops |
    | Logistician | +50% supply loads, served first |
    | Survivor | half the chance to die with the flagship |
    | Conqueror | captures twice as fast, half the resentment |

  - A flagship counts as Flagship counts as (3) ships for everything: Voronoi weight, cops and garrison, stores and food, hull and rate of fire.
  - A captain commands Command (10) escorts. More are allowed, but each 100% over slows the fleet to half speed and adds 0.3 shock per maintenance phase.
  - If the flagship is destroyed, the fleet is shaken and becomes a garrison. The captain dies with a Captain death (50%) chance; otherwise they get a new flagship at home 60 s later and take command of the ships waiting there.
- **Garrisons:** a planet's new ship joins the ships already there: its garrison, or else the biggest of its owner's fleets resting at the planet. Under each planet, x/y gives the ships there (flagships count as 3) over the ships needed to keep its people calm.
- **Independent planets:** every planet starts with as many ships as its size; independents build up to 5 × size and only defend.
- **Fleets:** Blue and Red each start with a home planet and 1–8 fleets (Fleets slider) of 3 ships, the largest planet size, each with three supply ships (ammo, food, parts), out on the home planet's highways. Every Blue or Red fleet takes orders unless routed. RTS controls: left-drag a box to select ships (or click a fleet; Shift adds, Esc clears), then right-click the map. Several selected fleets each take their own shortest highway route and merge into the biggest where they arrive. Part of a fleet splits off as a new fleet first. One whole fleet just moves. Both sides are controlled by hand.
- **One ship ECS:** every ship on the map is an entity in the battle's ECS world, and every fleet is one of its divisions, run by the battle's own `SimTick` systems (steering, blocking, gunnery, closing ranks, morale). Independents are a third side that fights Blue and Red but not each other. Map speeds come from the Fleet speed slider; the Combat speed slider shortens the reload.
- **Formations:** keys 1–6 set the selected fleets' formation (1 Line, 2 Wedge, 3 Crescent, 4 Echelon L, 5 Echelon R, 6 Column).
- **Combat:** fleets in range fight with the battle's rules: gun range and cone, front, flank and rear hit chances, range falloff, reload and hull. Ships never move into an enemy's space, and idle fleets turn to face the nearest enemy. A beaten fleet turns Shaken (gold, fires slower), then Routed (grey): it stops taking orders and flees off the map toward its home edge.
- **Capture:** a planet changes hands once its garrison is gone and a single enemy side has held it for 3 s (a ring shows the progress).

- **View:** the map renders at a fixed 1440×800 through a camera, so it stays sharp at any map size. The wheel over the map zooms toward the cursor (up to 8×), middle-drag pans, and the Zoom −, Zoom + and Fit buttons do the same.

Tests: `cargo test prototypes`. `cargo run --release --example prototype01_dump -- <dir>` plays three minutes and writes frames, a 4× close-up of each, and render timings.

## Hex board — BattleStars v0.2.1

The **Hex board →** button on the start page opens the alternating-activation hex rules, the one design that passed a paper playtest. Rules: `src/board/` (headless, on its own ECS `World`, squadrons are entities). Page: `src/game/board_page.rs`.

- 27×27 hex board, 5–12 squadrons a side (strength 4 each), one flagship per side; nine scenarios (Spindle vs Wide Line, supply handicaps, flagship hunt, …).
- Play Blue, Play Red, hotseat or spectate (Step / Auto). Deploy in the scenario's formation or place squadrons by hand.
- Click one of your squadrons to activate it, then Q/E turn, W forward, S back (full MP only), F or click a red-outlined enemy to fire, Space ends. Within 4 hexes of your flagship (tinted) you may move AND fire; outside it, move OR fire.
- Fire: range 3, forward arc, line of sight blocked by any squadron; to-hit 5+ front, 4+ flank, 3+ rear. Hits cost strength and force morale checks; Shaken squadrons fire half dice and won't close; Routed ones flee off their own edge. A fleet breaks after losing half; otherwise turn 15 ends it on surviving strength.
- AIs: doctrine (keeps its flagship back, faces threats, prefers flank shots) and the original baseline.

Tests: `cargo test board`.

## Voronoi labs

The **Voronoi labs →** button on the start page opens a separate page with every lab, its controls and its image. Labs only render while that page is open. A shared **Config** at the top (seed, site count, distance metric, Lloyd rounds) feeds all nine labs, after the gamegeniuslab tutorial *Voronoi diagrams in game development*:

1. grow the seeds;
2. nearest site;
3. owner map;
4. Delaunay & exact cells;
5. Lloyd relaxation;
6. biome island;
7. faction territories;
8. cell noise;
9. paint the island.

The code is in `src/voronoi/`. It's pure functions with its own tests (`cargo test voronoi`). `cargo run --release --example voronoi_dump -- <dir> [seed] [sites]` writes each lab as a PPM. **Fleet battle & campaign →** opens the game menu.

**New campaign** on the menu starts a turn-based war over a galaxy split by an impassable nebula. Two corridors cross it: a fortress in the north, a neutral trade world in the south ([docs/campaign-rules.md](docs/campaign-rules.md)).

* You command the Meridian Compact's fleets, each under an admiral, against the AI Hesper Throne.
* Each turn you send fleets to systems. Wherever fleets meet, you **fight** the battle on the battle screen, **auto-resolve** it with the same simulation, or **withdraw**.
* **Fleet sizes persist:** losses and damage carry over between battles.
* Held systems pay **ships every turn**, but only while supplied, meaning connected to your capital through your own systems. Fleets that push too far beyond that lose supply and fight worse.
* Damaged ships repair at home.
* Winning admirals are promoted and gain traits (Gunnery, Steadfast, Maneuver, Logistician).
* Take the enemy capital to win. The campaign autosaves every turn.

This started as a port of the [BattleStars](https://github.com/tharak/BattleStars) hex board (rules v0.2.1, still in this repo's history). Playing it showed that 9 squadrons a side, each destroyed in 2–3 shots, could not give a formation time to matter. So the game is now a continuous 2D simulation of every ship ([docs/fleet-rules.md](docs/fleet-rules.md)).

## How to play

For a single battle, set up a **Skirmish** on the menu: which side you command (or spectate), each side's formation, the number of squadrons (1–10, plus the flagship's squadron), the ships in each squadron (10–100), and the enemy AI. When you command a fleet, the battle starts paused so you can give orders first.

| Input | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Click a ship, or a row in the division list | Select its division (`Tab` cycles your divisions) |
| Right-click the map (or **Move…**, then click/tap) | Move there; drag while holding the button to set the facing |
| Click an enemy ship with your division selected | Engage that enemy division |
| **Hold / Advance / Charge / Withdraw** (`H` `G` `C` `X`) | Orders |
| Formation buttons (`F` cycles) | Line, Wedge, Crescent, Echelon L/R, Column |
| Drag / arrows / WASD, wheel / pinch, `+` `-` | Pan and zoom |
| `Space`, `1`–`4` | Pause, and speed 1× 2× 4× 8× |

* Ships stop short of enemy ships (a quarter of weapon range) instead of colliding. To get through a line you must destroy the ships in the way.
* A division that loses ships quickly turns Shaken, then Routs. Staying inside the flagship's command circle steadies it.
* There is no hard break point: the more of a fleet is lost, the shakier all its divisions get, until they rout one by one. Fleets run away or die. Killing the enemy flagship shocks its whole fleet and can set off a cascade of routs.

## How an arrow breaks a line

The breakthrough is not a scripted rule. It comes out of the simulation:

1. **Concentrated fire.** Ships in an Arrow (or Crescent) fleet near its centre shoot the reachable enemy closest to that centre, so the arrow pours its fire onto the few ships at its point. Other formations shoot whatever is nearest.
1. **No firing through friends.** A ship can't shoot if one of its own is in the way. A line's staggered second rank fires through the gaps, but a wedge's or column's inner rows are masked, so depth buys staying power, not extra guns.
1. **Short range.** Guns have a short range and fire only in a forward cone. Only the part of a line facing the arrow's tip can shoot at it; the rest of the line is out of the fight until it wheels.
2. **Depth.** A wedge closes ranks as it loses ships, so it always has a full front. A two-rank line keeps its holes.
3. **Blocking.** Ships stop a quarter of weapon range short of any enemy and never push through, so the arrow grinds into the line by destroying the ships in its way.
4. **Getting behind.** Once through, the arrow fires into rear arcs (40% to hit, against 12% from the front). The line's ships cannot turn far enough to answer until their division wheels.
5. **Morale.** Being broken through, plus the sudden losses, shakes and routs the broken divisions.

The counter is to put flanks on the arrow, for example with a crescent or by committing the reserve.

## Balance runs

`cargo run --release --bin sim -- 25 240` plays every scenario AI-vs-AI, with each fleet taking each seat half the time. Each cell gives the first-named fleet's win %, and the battle length in minutes with contact at about 0:48. 50 games per cell:

| Scenario | Commander AI both sides | Formation only (opening orders) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Arrow vs Line | **70%**, 3.2 min | 100%, on time |
| Line vs Line | 58%, 3.9 min | 44%, 3.6 min |
| Crescent vs Arrow | 50%, 3.0 min | 12%, 6.7 min |
| Echelon vs Line | 44%, 3.8 min | 16%, 3.9 min |
| Columns vs Line | 80%, 2.8 min | 96%, 5.0 min |
| Low supply (Red fires 8% slower) | 80%, 4.0 min | 94%, 3.5 min |

* With commanders on both sides, the arrow beats the line and the even matchups come out roughly even.
* "Formation only" means no division ever changes its opening order. That shows the raw shapes. The arrow wins on time there because it punches through and the line never turns to face it. Columns win for the same reason.
* Hundreds of ships average out the dice, so a small edge (like 8% slower fire) decides most battles.
* **Known weaknesses:**
  * With weapon range 150 and a 37.5 stopping distance, the crescent no longer reliably beats the arrow (50%), and deep columns beat lines (80%).
  * The commander AI loses Line vs Line to a formation-only line (34%). Its flanking moves cost it more than they gain.
  * Both need another tuning pass.

## Campaign balance

`cargo run --release --bin campaign_sim -- 16` plays whole wars with the AI on both sides. Over 16 wars:
* The sides split 6–10.
* Wars last 90 turns on average, with about 90 battles.
* Some end with a capital falling by turns 20–60. Others see-saw over the corridors until the 120-turn limit.

## Architecture

```
src/
  sim/                headless simulation; no rendering, no input
    components.rs     Ship, Pos, Vel, Heading, Side, InDivision, Slot, Weapon, FleetFlagship, Division
    formation.rs      slot layouts per formation
    spatial.rs        uniform-grid spatial hash (neighbours, targeting, centroids)
    systems.rs        the SimTick schedule: steering, blocking, fire, close ranks, morale, breakthroughs, victory
    ai.rs             the division-level commander AI
    scenario.rs       fleet shapes and scenarios
    battle.rs         settings, Tuning (every number), Battle state, SimEvent messages
    rng.rs            seeded Mulberry32
  campaign/           turn-based strategic layer, headless
    map.rs            the galaxy: systems, lanes, the nebula and its two corridors
    state.rs          Campaign (systems, fleets, admirals, pending battles), serde
    turn.rs           movement and battle detection; capture, supply, income, repair, replacements
    battle.rs         campaign fleets -> sim Forces (with admiral traits and supply), Report -> losses and retreats
    ai.rs             the rival power's strategy
    save.rs           JSON save in localStorage / ~/.local/share
  game/               the Bevy app
    flow.rs           start/restart, applies orders, steps the sim at the chosen speed, log lines
    view.rs           ship meshes (shared mesh and materials, batched), labels, lasers, explosions, order overlays
    camera.rs         pan and zoom around the side panel
    input.rs          mouse, touch and keyboard
    model.rs          per-frame UiModel read model
    ui.rs             menu, side panel, division list, orders, log, rules, result overlay
    campaign_ui.rs    galaxy map, fleet orders, pre-battle card, auto-resolve, campaign panel model
    autoplay.rs       scripted native smoke run that saves screenshots (dev only)
  bin/sim.rs          battle balance runner
  bin/campaign_sim.rs AI-vs-AI campaign runner
examples/trace.rs     battle timeline, with optional PPM snapshots (TRACE_PPM=dir)
examples/campaign_trace.rs   AI-vs-AI war, turn by turn
```

Every ship is an entity, and so is every division. The simulation is ordinary ECS systems in a custom `SimTick` schedule with a fixed step. The game runs that schedule from `FixedUpdate` 0–8 times per step, and the tests and balance runner call it directly on a bare `World`. A seed always replays the same battle.

## Develop

```bash
cargo test --lib                          # sim + campaign tests (formations, blocking, morale, supply, income, battles, saves)
cargo run                                 # native window
cargo run --release --bin sim -- 25 240   # battle balance table
cargo run --release --bin sim -- --rust 25 240   # regenerate src/sim/odds.rs (simulated win rates per scenario)
cargo run --release --bin campaign_sim -- 16   # AI-vs-AI wars
cargo run --release --example trace -- 0 c 1   # timeline of Arrow vs Line, commander AI, seed 1
SG_AUTOPLAY=/tmp/shots cargo run          # one battle per formation (must not draw) + campaign map, saves PNGs
./scripts/build-web.sh                    # wasm bundle into dist/ (python3 -m http.server -d dist)
```

GitHub Actions runs the tests, builds the WebAssembly bundle and deploys `dist/` to GitHub Pages on every push to `main`.
